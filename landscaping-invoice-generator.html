How invoice totals are calculated

subtotal → discount → tax → shipping → total → balance due

Each line item's total is quantity multiplied by unit price. Those line totals are added up into a subtotal. Your discount — either a percentage or a fixed amount — is subtracted from the subtotal next. Tax is then calculated only on the items you've marked as taxable, using the discounted amount, so a discount always reduces the tax you owe too, not just the pre-tax price. Shipping or any extra charge is added after tax, since it typically isn't part of the taxable goods or service. The result is your total. Subtracting anything the customer has already paid gives the balance due.

How Landscapings typically invoice

field-tested guidance, built into this preset

Landscaping billing splits into two common patterns: a one-off project (a new bed, sod installation, hardscaping) priced with labor and materials as separate lines, and recurring service (weekly or bi-weekly mowing) usually billed as a flat per-visit or per-month rate. Materials for larger jobs — mulch, plants, pavers — are typically itemized so the customer can see the cost breakdown, especially on higher-ticket projects.

Tips for this preset

  • For recurring lawn care, note the frequency in the item description (e.g. "Lawn mowing — weekly, visit 6 of 12") since the invoice itself doesn't track a schedule.
  • Itemize materials individually on larger design/install jobs (mulch, plants, pavers) rather than a single "materials" line, especially when a customer has asked to see the breakdown.
  • Seasonal work (spring cleanup, fall leaf removal) is often clearer as its own invoice rather than folded into a standing lawn care contract.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Billing a seasonal one-off job on the same invoice as a standing recurring contract, which confuses the payment schedule.
  • Not itemizing plant and material costs on larger design/install jobs where customers expect to see the breakdown.
  • Leaving off which visit number a recurring invoice represents, making it hard to track against the season's contract.

Landscaping Invoice Generator FAQ

How do I bill for a recurring lawn care contract?

Create a separate invoice per billing period and note the visit or period in the description (e.g. "bi-weekly service — May"). This tool generates individual invoices; it doesn't automate a recurring schedule.

Should I separate materials from labor on a landscaping design job?

Generally yes, especially for larger design/install projects — customers commonly expect to see plants, mulch and hardscaping materials broken out from labor.

Is landscaping labor taxable?

That depends on your state, province or country's rules for services versus goods — check with your local tax authority. You can mark labor and materials lines as taxable independently.

Is this invoice generator free to use?

Yes, it is completely free with no sign-up required.

Can I download the invoice as a PDF?

Yes. Use the Print / Save as PDF button, then choose "Save as PDF" as the destination in your browser's print dialog.

Does this tool store or upload my data?

No. All calculations run entirely in your browser, and nothing you enter is ever sent to a server or stored, unless you turn on the optional "remember in this browser" setting, which only saves data locally on your own device.
